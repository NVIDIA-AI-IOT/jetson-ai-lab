# Model-family visual system prototype

This prototype separates **model metadata**, **family-level visual identity**, and **deterministic visual output**.

The normative contribution contract is [`docs/model-family-visual-guidelines.md`](./model-family-visual-guidelines.md). Every human or agent adding a family must follow it and supply the manifest rationale enforced by `npm run check:model-visuals`.

The active family artwork is deterministic WebGL2/GLSL rather than uploaded key art. The shader is the source of truth; committed WebPs are reproducible first-paint and failure fallbacks from the same renderer. Independent SVG interpretations, candidate sheets, and generated companion logos are not shipped.

## Proposed ownership model

- Each model can opt into a family visual with `visual_family: qwen` in its Markdown frontmatter.
- A family with more than one treatment must also declare an explicit `visual_variant` in frontmatter. The pair selects both live shader defaults and generation-specific fallback assets; model names, slugs, and catalog grouping are never parsed to infer visual generations. Content validation rejects missing variants, fallback mappings, or variants absent from the central defaults registry during the build.
- `src/data/modelVisuals.ts` is the runtime registry used by catalog cards and model detail pages.
- `src/data/modelVisualPresentation.ts` owns reviewed site-level presentation defaults such as catalog artwork intensity. These values are not user preferences; changing them requires code review.
- `src/data/modelShaderDefaults.json` is the single source of truth for every shader's runtime, fallback-capture, benchmark, and Visual Lab defaults. The manifest points to this file instead of duplicating tuned values.
- `artwork/model-family-visuals/manifest.json` records palette, mathematical grammar, generator revision, official references, and review status.
- `src/shaders/qwen-isometric.frag.glsl` defines Qwen's slowly drifting isometric slab field. Its center, scale, drift speed, and drift distance are exposed in the prototype.
- `src/shaders/gemma-streams.frag.glsl` keeps Gemma in one pale-blue system. Gemma 3 uses six quiet translucent streams; Gemma 4 increases curvature and layer count and adds sparse blue particulate highlights derived from its launch-art language. Speed, width, curvature, and intensity are exposed.
- Gemma uses its family name as text until an approved, locally distributable first-party identity asset is available. The repository does not ship a reconstructed Spark.
- `src/shaders/cosmos-galaxy.frag.glsl` rotates its complete logarithmic-arm and particle-clump field around the luminous core, then projects it through an adjustable oblique view. Arm width now extends to 4.0 to reduce unused black area without merely scaling the galaxy. The generation buttons keep Cosmos 2 predominantly green. Cosmos 3 adds a phase-displaced parallel violet spiral that fades in outside the core; hue-replacement compositing prevents overlap from bleaching the original yellow-green arms toward white. `Cosmos 3 violet bands` exposes its intensity without affecting Cosmos 2. Particle breakup now uses bilinearly interpolated galaxy-space value noise instead of discontinuous floor/hash cells, greatly reducing rotation-induced sub-pixel flicker. `Particle twinkle` defaults to 0.08 and can be set to 0 for maximum temporal stability. Core position, galaxy scale, arm width, brightness, view tilt, view direction, initial galaxy rotation, and animation speed are exposed. At the default speed a revolution takes about two minutes; maximum speed reduces this to roughly 24 seconds.
- Discrete visual generations use segmented buttons rather than sliders: Qwen 3/3.5/3.8, Gemma 3/4, Cosmos 2/3, and Nemotron 3/3.5. Each button applies a reviewed preset to the same family shader; these are concept treatments, not claims of official generation-specific brand art.
- Nemotron compact identity uses the official NVIDIA-hosted neural-network cube SVG shown beside “NVIDIA Nemotron” on the NVIDIA Developer AI Models page. The close-up DLP-like micromirror field is deliberately kept as a separate background treatment.
- `src/shaders/nemotron-microcube.frag.glsl` renders a close-up oblique surface of individually separated, transparent DLP-like square mirrors. Two analytic travelling waves form a real Z height field. Every cell center is displaced in the screen projection, while the local height derivatives deform its projected square basis and define its 3D normal. A 3×3 neighborhood search resolves tiles that move outside their resting raster cells; projected contact shadows make the lift visible. Two slowly moving point lights are evaluated against those derived normals, with no pre-painted highlight inside a mirror. Color is reserved for thin rims and directional specular reflections rather than opaque tile fills. No enclosing cube is drawn. Surface position, zoom, angle, mirror density, wavelength, displacement, tilt, and speed are exposed.
- `src/shaders/liquid-decision.frag.glsl`, `llama-orbit.frag.glsl`, `gpt-oss-harmony.frag.glsl`, `minimax-recursion.frag.glsl`, and `flux3-action-field.frag.glsl` extend the same runtime/fallback contract to Liquid AI d1, Meta Llama, OpenAI gpt-oss, MiniMax M2, and FLUX.3 Action. Llama's orbit remains a provisional Llama-only treatment. gpt-oss uses a generic strongly oblique monochrome plane of gray tiles and deliberately avoids architecture claims. Their model-specific rationale is mandatory in the manifest rather than inferred from the final pixels.
- Muse Glimmer and Ministral 3 use `LayeredModelShaderCanvas.astro`. A reduced-resolution offscreen framebuffer carries only the field background, dust, and broad halos: 0.10× for Muse and 0.5× for Ministral by default. Muse then draws 34 moving edge quads, 16 node quads, and 170 burst/trail quads from compact per-frame instance buffers at full output resolution. Ministral draws its 108 warm rectangles as full-resolution instanced quads. The prototype exposes each background scale down to 0.10× and gives both treatments separate 0.50×–1.00× foreground-resolution controls plus post-render soft focus. This replaces the former per-pixel 32-edge/5-trail graph search and 9×12 rectangle search without lowering the shipping resolution of fine lines or boundaries.
- Every prototype Tune panel appends the same `Dark overlay` control. It adjusts a black DOM presentation layer above the generated visual, leaving shader colors and measured WebGL execution unchanged. This is the preferred way to compare dimmer page treatments consistently across families.
- Every Tune panel can preview or copy a Shadertoy-ready Image shader with the current control values embedded. The exporter removes WebGL2 declarations, maps time and the cropped JAL viewport to Shadertoy built-ins, and does not publish anything externally. Muse Glimmer and Ministral 3 are multi-pass renderers, so their Shadertoy exports are explicitly labeled single-pass approximations; the adjacent download contains the complete background, instanced-geometry, and composite source bundle for local inspection.
- `/model-visuals-prototype/` is an internal Visual Lab route available through `npm run dev`; the production build hook deliberately excludes it and its save API from generated routes and output. It begins with a switchable full-width model-page hero for placement review. The selected family's existing live showcase canvas is moved into this preview rather than allocating a duplicate WebGL context, so generation and tuning changes remain exact. An optional model-information card is populated from a representative model's real frontmatter and exposes composition conflicts such as an unsafe shader X offset. The showcase then presents every registered family—including Qwen—through the same hero, generation, tuning/export, catalog-crop, identity, and rationale structure; no family receives a one-off presentation layout.
- Each Tune panel reads its saved values from `src/data/modelShaderDefaults.json`. **Reset to saved defaults** restores that reviewed source state. **Save current values as defaults** first shows an exact parameter diff, then uses a same-origin development-only endpoint to validate the complete numeric parameter set and atomically update the JSON file. Astro reloads the page after the write and restores a success notice. The operation deliberately creates neither a commit nor a production write path, so the resulting source diff remains subject to ordinary review.

- Nemotron 3.5 defaults to the reviewed close treatment: density 6, wave length 0.91, Z displacement 4.40, mirror tilt 1.48, and 2 px soft focus. At this low density and scale ≥1, the inverse-projected mirror lookup evaluates a 3×3 neighborhood (9 candidates) instead of 5×5 (25 candidates), while higher density or zooming out automatically restores the wider perimeter search. The fully dark left strip also exits before surface reconstruction. Static fallbacks receive the same 2 px soft focus offline.
- `src/components/ModelShaderCanvas.astro` is the shared live WebGL2 runner. Every live treatment is capped at 20 fps, renders only while visible, honors reduced-motion preferences, and reveals a static WebP fallback if WebGL is unavailable. The live canvas occupies only the rightmost 76% of its hero and maps that cropped buffer back into the original full-width shader coordinate system, preserving composition while eliminating about 24% of fragment work. The full-width static fallback remains beneath it so the copy area retains a quiet, non-animated continuation. Gemma 4 uses a 0.5 resolution scale; Gemma 3 uses 0.7. Nemotron defaults to 1.0 because its fine mirror grid exposes upscaling artifacts; the prototype Tune panel exposes both render resolution and optional post-render soft focus for comparison. `src/components/ShaderControls.astro` provides the prototype-only parameter surface.
- gpt-oss also defaults to a 1.0 render scale because its perspective grid compresses many tiles near the horizon. Its Tune panel exposes render resolution and post-render soft focus independently; the default 0.30 px focus lightly suppresses distant-grid aliasing without changing geometry. Deterministic events affect only the foreground: selected tiles remain flat and briefly take on a restrained launch-blue tint. Cadence now changes the interval without shortening pulse duration, defaults to 4×, and can be tuned through 10×. Independent distance fog progressively dissolves sub-pixel tiles into the dark background. Once a coherent region falls below the visibility threshold, the shader returns before tile IDs, reflections, seams, and pulses are evaluated, so fog now reduces both visible aliasing and far-field arithmetic work.

- Ministral 3 exposes render resolution and post-render soft focus independently. It retains the existing 0.75× resolution and 0 px blur by default because hard pixel-step edges are part of the treatment, but the prototype can be tuned through 0.5–1.5× resolution and 0–2.5 px blur to compare edge quality and fragment cost.
- Model names and official marks remain accessible HTML or reviewed official assets; they are never baked into procedural backgrounds.
- Families with an established and approved mark keep it as a separately sourced `official` brand layer. The procedural background is decorative and is never presented as a logo.
- `npm run build:model-visuals` reproduces the offline WebGL captures. Previous raster explorations, candidate sheets, strips, and SVG intermediates are not active assets and are not committed.

## Asset contract

| Variant | Output | Intended surface |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `hero` | 1920 × 720 WebP | Deterministic model-detail fallback |
| `catalog` | 960 × 540 WebP | Deterministic catalog fallback |
| `icon` | source-defined | Separately reviewed official identity only |

Hero and catalog fallbacks remain independently addressable and are rendered from the same checked-in shader used live. An official icon retains its original geometry and is never regenerated or baked into the background. If redistribution has not been reviewed, omit the icon and use text.

Catalog family headings place an identity mark in the same fixed square optical frame only when local redistribution has been explicitly approved. This change set ships NVIDIA-owned Nemotron and Cosmos marks only. All third-party families use accessible provider/model text until a reviewed, locally distributable first-party asset is available; remote linking, copied favicons, and inferred launch-partner permission are not substitutes for approval. Cropping an approved transparent canvas is allowed; redrawing, stretching, or changing the mark itself is not.

The shipping check keeps an explicit redistribution-reviewed identity allowlist in `scripts/check-model-family-visuals.mjs`. Adding an icon file or labelling it `official` in the manifest is intentionally insufficient; a newly approved identity asset must also be added to that allowlist.

The Models directory uses the three views for distinct jobs. **Cards** are a stable lineage map: generation-specific groups such as Qwen 3/3.5/3.6/3.8, Gemma 3/4, and Cosmos 1/2/3 collapse into one family card, show at most four matching models, and expand on demand. Cards follow an editorial capability order rather than an alphabetic sort and use a dense masonry grid to avoid empty row space. **Compact** keeps every generation-specific group visible for scanning, while **Table** remains the exhaustive sortable comparison view.

Catalog artwork intensity is a reviewed site default from `src/data/modelVisualPresentation.ts`, not a user-facing slider or browser preference. The sole user-facing visibility preference is **Model visual On/Off**, stored under `jetson-ai-lab:model-visuals`; the catalog and every model-detail page read the same value so disabling a visual on one model page disables model-family artwork everywhere. When enabled, Compact view exposes only a **Model visual style** choice—**By model** or **By family**. The style control disappears when Model visual is off and cannot override the shared visibility preference.

When an official or established mark is composed of very fine strokes that disappear against the catalog wash, preserve its geometry and add contrast outside the asset. Nemotron uses a near-black green-tinted tile with a restrained family-color border and one-pixel glow. Do not thicken or redraw source paths merely to make a catalog icon louder.

## Identity versus procedural decoration

An optional official icon answers “whose model family is this?” and remains a separate reviewed identity layer. The live shader supplies page-scale decoration and must not be presented as an official logo. For Liquid AI d1, for example, unequal ribbons converging over a broad sphere haze and resolving into probability traces are shader behavior, not an additional uploaded motif file or a claim about internal topology.

## Generation and review flow

1. Resolve the canonical model family and inspect official first-party sources for its established palette and mark.
2. Choose one minimal mathematical grammar: waves, arcs, a grid plus selection mask, convergence, or branching paths.
3. Record the grammar and revision in the manifest, then implement it as a checked-in WebGL2 shader or renderer.
4. Derive hero and catalog WebP fallbacks from that same shader. Change model generations through named parameters rather than a new hand-authored picture.
5. Keep an approved official icon separate. Never create a procedural companion logo; omit identity artwork when an approved first-party asset is unavailable.
6. Run `npm run dev`, tune in the internal `/model-visuals-prototype/` route, and use **Save current values as defaults** only after reviewing its diff. Then run `npm run build:model-visuals` so fallbacks use those same saved values. Inspect hero, card, and compact sizes. Do not expect the Lab or its save endpoint in `dist/` or a production deployment.
7. Review contrast, crop, recognizability, provenance, and whether every visible element can be explained by its parameters.
8. Change `reviewStatus` from `prototype` only after human approval.

The registry is deliberately more constrained than a collection of unrelated abstract card images: a family keeps one palette, visual grammar, icon policy, and provenance record across releases. Individual models may vary the composition later without losing family recognition.

## Fallback behavior

Models without `visual_family` continue to use the existing neutral black hero and text-only catalog presentation. This allows gradual adoption without requiring all families to receive artwork at once.
